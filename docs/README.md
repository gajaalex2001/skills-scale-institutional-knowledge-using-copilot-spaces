# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This directory contains OctoAcme's project management processes and role guidance. Use this README as the starting point to find the relevant workflow and understand how work moves from an idea through delivery and learning.

## Project lifecycle

1. **Initiation:** Validate the business need and measurable outcome, identify stakeholders, outline the timeline and initial risks, and agree on whether to proceed to planning.
2. **Planning:** Turn the approved initiative into a prioritized, estimated backlog with acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done. Confirm capacity, dependencies, milestones, the release plan, and the initial QA approach.
3. **Execution:** Deliver in small increments using the project board and agreed team cadence. Review and test work through pull requests and CI, demonstrate progress, track outcome metrics, and keep risks and blockers visible and escalated.
4. **Release:** Confirm acceptance criteria, CI and security checks, release notes, smoke tests, and rollback plans are ready. Deploy, verify the release, and communicate it to stakeholders and support.
5. **Close and improve:** After a sprint, release, milestone, or incident, capture what went well and what can improve. Assign owners and due dates to a small set of follow-up actions, track them, and measure their impact.

## Principles and roles

OctoAcme's approach is customer-first, iterative, based on clear ownership and evidence, and supportive of psychological safety and learning. The Project Manager coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, and communications; the Product Manager defines outcomes, prioritizes work, and measures success. Developers build and test increments, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input and approvals.

Teams use lightweight, visible artifacts—including a project one-pager, roadmap and release plan, backlog, acceptance criteria and Definition of Done, risk register, and retrospective actions—to maintain alignment. The specific communication cadence and team rhythm can be adjusted by agreement; see the overview and execution guides for the documented examples.
